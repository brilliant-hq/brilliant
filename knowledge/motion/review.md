# Motion: Review

A sheet proves the picture, a table proves the time. Look at both before calling a film done.

**The render.** `export` with `format: "mp4"` and `motion: "Launch"` plays the film `Motion/Launch.bm` beside the canvas the call names or at the project root, through the engine, the same frames as the canvas (glass, shaders, tokens). `motion: {film: "Launch", frames: [a, b]}` renders frames a up to b, `fps` overrides the header's, and `videoQuality` is `low`, `medium` or `high` (the default). A film renders whole into one mp4 up to 36000 frames (10 minutes at 60 fps), and the mp4 has no audio track: the runner mixes the sound. Size photos to their on-screen pixels.

**The answer.** The mp4 path, the contact sheet (24 frames tiled 6x4, time-stamped), the per-frame timing, and `matches` per transition: the names that tweened, those that dissolved in and out, a set's members, a pair that changed parents. An unintended dissolve is always in `matches`. `craft` lists every craft finding (`motion/craft`) with its line, on every call.

**The loop.** Render the contact pass, fix what the sheet shows off the grid, cramped or unreadable, render the clip, look again at full-resolution frames on the beats.

**The viewer.** A sheet and a table prove frames, a cold-viewer read proves the story (`motion/craft`, The process): run it on the animatic and on the cut. Then by eye: no hard cut inside one subject (move the camera instead), title clearance from its object (a title never crowds what it names), each claim's effect visible at phone size (Ami 11-10).

**The rhythm reads.** The runner (`reference/motion`) counts the tool rules before a full render and refuses on a fail, reads the timeline before any render and the pixels after, and names each finding with its element, frame, film line and fix: the clock, the rhythm strip, long moves, simultaneous starts, short rests, glides, off-accent phrases, dead time, the energy budget, blur, the camera window and runs, stutters and jump cuts, the drop, the riser, the end card and the mix.

**The write answer.** A `bm` fence through `edit` answers with a picture of the film, rendered inside an 8 s budget: a 960 px contact sheet of 24 tiles, a strip of at most 48 frames and a rhythm strip (a row per moving element, velocity as intensity, a tick per start, a dot per settle, the beats under it). A cut render says how many frames rendered of how many, one with no frame says so and names `export` with `motion`, the full render.

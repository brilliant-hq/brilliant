# Account and Project Settings

## Account (Settings tab)

The Account pane is the same on desktop and the web app; each section appears only when there is data behind it. It shows the signed-in identity (name, @handle, email) with two buttons on the right of that row: **open your profile** (globe, only when the account has a handle; desktop opens `brilliant.design/{handle}` in the browser, the web app switches to the profile view in place) and **sign out**. Plan, Usage and Subscription appear where entitlements exist, on both desktop and the web app (on the web they live in Settings > Plan & billing, with a signed-in platform session). A gifted plan reads "Gift, until <date>" when the gift has an end (and "Gift" when it does not), the thanks line names the same date, and when that date passes the account is back on Free with everything you made still yours. With a platform identity there is also a **Profile** section with the handle row: `@handle` at rest with a pencil to edit in place. Every account gets an auto-generated word-pair handle (like `@cozy-cactus`) at sign-up, nothing derived from the email is ever published, and it can be changed here any time. While typing, availability is checked live (taken/invalid verdicts inline); handles shorter than 6 characters are a paid tier (shown as a "Paid" chip up front, and as an upgrade hint if the server declines). Paid accounts also carry a blue badge (a blue check) on their public profile; it is derived live from the plan, so it disappears if the plan lapses. Published work lives at `brilliant.design/{handle}` (no `@` in the URL; the `@` is display convention only, and `/@{handle}` merely redirects); after a rename, old profile and project links redirect to the new handle until someone else claims the old name, and the old handle is reserved for you for 30 days.

## Project (Settings tab)

The project-scoped surface (publishing, sharing, cover, library) is the **Project tab in Settings**, third in the rail after Account and General (Cmd+, then Project, previously named "Sharing"). A published project heads the pane with its `@handle/project` path, the thing being shared. It only appears when a project context exists (a published project, or an unpublished desktop folder with a signed-in identity). Opened directly from the **Project** command, the account-avatar menu's "Project settings" row, or the copy-link button when the folder isn't published yet. Rows:

- **Publish / Published:** a settings row showing whether the project is live on the web; its title is state-derived, reading "Publish" until the project is live and "Published" once it is. Ticking it is how you publish: it never changes anything on the spot, it raises an "are you sure?" confirm on the notification rail that spells out exactly what the change means, and only that confirm's button acts. Once the project is published the row is a plain status line, "Published" with "This project is live on the web.", and two small buttons anyone can use (not just the owner, since both are read-only): **Copy link** (copies the project's public URL, the icon flips to a check for a moment) and **Open in web** (opens the public page in your browser). Unpublishing lives in the Danger zone (below).
- **Visibility** (project owner only): a toggle between public and private. Flipping it doesn't change anything on the spot: like the Published row, it raises an "are you sure?" confirm on the notification rail first, and only that confirm's button acts. Going private warns that public links stop working immediately (people you've invited keep access); going public warns that anyone with the link can view it and it appears on your public profile. Private is a paid feature: if the account isn't on a paid plan, the server declines and shows a "Paid" badge, and choosing **Upgrade** opens the upgrade flow in place rather than sending you to a pricing page. A Personal purchase completes inside the app (a checkout page on the same overlay, hosting Paddle) and the plan flips live, or you can open the same checkout in your browser from the footer (where your browser's password manager or Apple Pay work), and the app still updates itself when the payment goes through; while that checkout page is open the editor toolbars step aside so the page has the screen to itself, and they return the moment you close it; a switch between paid rungs happens in place on the plan sheet, and that in-place switch is for a live subscription you bought: if your plan came another way (a gift or a comped plan, which has no subscription behind it to change), choosing a rung opens the checkout and buys it, the same door the editor takes, rather than sending you to a settings page with nothing to manage; creating a team finishes in your browser and the app updates itself the moment the plan flips. On desktop the wait stays honest about slow payments: if confirmation takes a while it says "taking longer than expected" and keeps checking on its own, with a **Check again** button to nudge it; and if your browser will not open, it shows the checkout link as copyable text (with a retry) so you can still finish. Flipping to private stops any outstanding share links from working (private means private).
- **Audience** (project owner only): a toggle between showing your project live and showing published checkpoints only. Like Visibility, flipping it doesn't change anything on the spot: it raises an "are you sure?" confirm on the notification rail first, and only that confirm's button acts. Switching to checkpoints only warns that visitors see your latest checkpoint instead of the live canvas and that anyone viewing live right now is disconnected (links keep working, they show the checkpoint face); switching back to live warns that visitors see your canvas live as you edit. Showing checkpoints only is a paid feature: on a free plan the server declines and shows a "Paid" badge with the same in-place **Upgrade** flow as Visibility. With checkpoints only on, anyone below editor (viewers and signed-out visitors) sees ONE checkpoint, the latest one (your newest checkpoint on the main line, never a draft's): older checkpoints are not listed and cannot be opened or copied, no list of what changed is shown, and nothing says what changed live since. You and your editors keep the whole history; with the project live, viewers see its whole history too. In the Checkpoints tab such a visitor sees that one checkpoint as a single row, with no live-work row above it and no preview (the canvas they are looking at already is that checkpoint). On a live project a viewer or visitor can open any checkpoint read-only: its bar offers **Back to live** and **Open a copy** (a project of their own made from that checkpoint; signed out, it opens as a playground copy) in place of Restore, and the create button stays disabled for them.
- **Access** (owner only): who's on the project, laid out exactly like Settings → Models → Custom providers. Everyone already on it lists first, one compact row each: a green dot, the name, their @handle, the role dropdown and a remove button (the owner's row is read-only). Pending invitations follow in the same shape with a gray dot and their state ("viewer, Pending, expires in 5d"; they last 14 days) plus a re-invite button that replaces the earlier email's link. Last comes the add row: type an email, pick the role beside it (**viewer**, read-only, the default, or **editor**, can change the project), press the checkmark (or Enter) to send, or the x to clear the row. A successful invite says nothing, the new pending row above the field is the confirmation; anything that goes wrong (already a collaborator, a refused address, an invite created whose email couldn't be sent) shows in red under the field. The invitation email carries an accept link: opened signed-out it asks the person to sign in with the invited email first, then access activates on the spot (a brand-new account signing up with that email gets it automatically); the invite only works for the email it was sent to. Rows with extra to say, such as an editor grant currently held at view-only, carry an expand button that reveals it. Change a collaborator's role or remove them any time; changes take effect immediately. Inviting works on any plan, but editing a **private** project needs a paid plan on the invitee's side: a free invitee gets the project read-only with an upgrade hint until they upgrade (no re-invite needed after upgrading).

- **Cover** and **Library** close the tab, in that order (viewers see no Library section, editors one line; `reference/libraries/publishing`), on published and unpublished projects alike. Cover manages the one image the project presents itself with. Its "Choose canvas or frame" door swaps the pane in place for a list of the project's canvases (Back or Escape returns): each row is a 16:9 thumbnail, the canvas name with its folder under it, and two actions on the right, "Use as cover" and "Choose a frame". Clicking anywhere on a row uses that canvas as the cover; "Choose a frame" goes one level down to a grid of that canvas's top-level frames, led by a "Whole canvas" tile so the whole-canvas choice stays one click away. The list states its count, and a project with more than eight canvases gets a filter field above the rows. Library is where a project is marked as a library and releases are cut; see `reference/libraries`.

Viewers and editors see only the Published row, and cannot act on it. An agent you are working with can change Published, Visibility, Audience and the project's name for you on your word (an owner's project only): it tells you what the change means first and then makes it, with no confirm card; Access stays yours to change here.

**Project settings in the new interface.** The rail's **Project** tab lists the pages (General, Access, Libraries, Fonts, Design system, Danger zone) and shows the chosen one in the main area; every project setting the website has lives here too, and both read and write the same project. **General** leads with **Display name** (free text up to 100 characters, shown on cards and headers; the address stays the same; clear it to show the address name again), then **Name** (the address, the `{project}` in `brilliant.design/{handle}/{project}`: type a new one and press Enter to rename the address; it is lowercased with spaces turned into dashes, old links keep working and redirect to the new address, and a name the server will not take shows its reason under the field. Only the project's owner, or a team admin on a team project, can rename it; for anyone else, and on a project that is not published yet, the field is greyed out with a line saying why. It never renames the folder on your computer: rename that from the project's tab or its Home tile), **Description**, and **Topics** (up to 5; type one and press Enter, or several separated by commas; they are lowercased with spaces turned into dashes, and a topic that still is not letters, numbers and single dashes is refused with the reason; click a topic chip to remove it). **Access** adds **Share links** after Visibility and Audience for the owner: on a private project, **New link** makes a read-only link anyone can open without signing in (a paid feature; a free plan opens the upgrade sheet), each link shows its `brilliant.design/s/...` address, when it was created and how many times it was viewed, with **Copy link** and **Revoke** (Revoke asks first, and only that one link stops working). On a public project the card says share links appear once it is private. Editors and viewers see Access read-only: the visibility as a sentence, the people on the project (an editor sees everyone, a viewer the owner) and "Only the owner can invite people". **Danger zone** starts with **Unpublish** (owner only, desktop app): it makes the project local-only. Press **Unpublish…** and type the project name; the confirm reads "Make <project> local-only? It stays on this Mac and is removed from Brilliant's servers. Collaborators and share links lose access." ("this PC" on Windows, "this computer" on Linux). If the project is not on this computer yet it is downloaded first, then every file is checked against the server; the row shows each step ("Downloading 3 of 12…", "Checking 5 of 12…") with a **Cancel** button. Only when every file is here and matches is the project removed from Brilliant's servers; from that moment the button reads **Unpublishing…** and can no longer be cancelled. When it finishes a notice says "<project> is local-only" ("It's on this Mac and no longer on Brilliant's servers."), and the folder stays as a local project. If anything stops it (a file missing or newer on the server, a failed download, Cancel, the project changing meanwhile, or recent edits from a collaborator not saved on the server yet) nothing is unpublished and a notice says "Couldn't download everything. Nothing was unpublished." with the reason. Then **Transfer to a team** for the owner of a personal project who belongs to a team: pick a team you administer, press **Transfer**, and type the project name to confirm; transfers are one-way and the address moves to the team. A team you are only a member of cannot receive it, and a free team cannot take a private project. **Delete project…** (owner only) deletes the project from the cloud for everyone, its canvases and its history, exactly like the website's Delete: type the project name to confirm. The project's tab closes and it leaves Home's cloud list; a folder copy on this computer keeps all its files and stays as a local project (to remove a local folder, use **Delete project…** on its Home tile). Support can restore a deleted cloud project for 30 days.

**The Design system page.** With the project's `Styles/default.ds` in place, the **Design system** page is a viewer of that design system laid out like Settings: its own rail on the left, a group named after the design system (for example **default**) holding one page per collection (**Colours**, **Roles**, **Scales**, **Type**, **Effects**), and a **System** group with **Modes** and **View as file**. **Colours** shows each brand colour with its value, its 9-step ramp with **Soft**, **Standard** or **Bold** at the end of the Ramp line, right above the steps (how far the ramp's light and deep ends reach; point at it for that line), and how it behaves in dark mode (**Auto** means dark follows light, mirrored for you; picking a colour of its own for dark is coming soon, so its **Change…** is not available yet). Colours the file lists under `pin:` come first and those under `hide:` come last. **Roles** shows what designs bind (`color.surface`, `color.text.primary`, ...) with two swatches, the colour it really is in light and in dark, and the ramp step it points at (dark: auto, or its own dark step). **Scales** lists spacing, radius and stroke steps with their values. **Type** shows each font family with **Change…**, which opens the font list; the font you pick is saved to the `.ds` right away and a toast offers **Undo**. Each text style is drawn in its own style (its size, weight and family) with its facts under it (`typography.h1 · font.size.3xl · bold · 1.2`), and every font scale is listed: **Sizes**, **Weights**, **Line heights** and **Letter spacing**. The sliders button on a text style opens its **Style properties**: Family (click to pick a font), Weight, Size, Line height and Letter spacing; type a number, or a token name such as `3xl` or `bold`, and press Enter. **Effects** shows each shadow as a tile previewed on a card; click one to edit its layers below it: **X** and **Y** (offset), **B** (blur) and **S** (spread), the colour swatch (the colour picker; the shadow follows while you drag and saves when you let go) and the opacity. Hover a layer for its **…** menu: **Duplicate layer** and **Remove layer** (a shadow keeps at least one layer). **Add layer** copies the last layer, and the shadow's **…** menu has **Duplicate**. **Add shadow** creates a new soft one-layer shadow named `shadow.new` with its name ready to type: type a name (for example `card`, which becomes `shadow.card`) and press Enter. Every edit saves to the `.ds` right away with **Undo** in the toast. **Modes** lists, per mode, only the tokens that have their own value there (**Differs in dark · N**, each with its light and dark swatches and an **Auto** button that removes its own dark value so it follows light again, with **Undo** in the toast), then **Follows light** with one **N tokens** row whose **Show** lists the rest. Each page also has its own search field at the top right, before **View as file**, which filters that page only (see Design systems, Searching a design system page). **Search tokens** at the top of the rail works like Settings search: the rail keeps only the pages with a match, each with its count, and matching rows are highlighted on the page; a search with no match says **No matches**. A page with more than 40 tokens offers **All | Changed | Unused** at the top, each with its count: Changed keeps only what you changed (from the Brilliant default when the design system started from it, otherwise from the design system's first committed version; no Changed when the project has no history to compare with), and Unused keeps only the tokens no layer in the project uses (open or closed canvases; a component instance counts on its main component, except where the instance overrides the token, which counts on the instance). Unused appears once every canvas has been counted, and a cloud project without a local folder does not offer it. On the Unused filter, **Delete all N…** deletes every unused token the file declares, in one step you can undo. Every token row has a **…** menu (it shows when you point at the row; right-click the row to open it): **Rename…** renames the token in the file and on every layer that uses it, including inside component instances and on canvases that are not open; **Where used** lists the canvases that use it; **Duplicate** copies it under a free name; **Delete…** removes it, and every layer that used it keeps today's colour (or value) as a raw value, no longer tied to a token, as does any role that pointed at it. Each of these is one step you can undo. **View as file** (on every page header and in the rail) opens the `.ds` text in the code editor. Clicking any `.ds` in Files opens these same pages for that file in the main area (see Design systems). When the project's design system is a library's, the pages show it read-only with a line naming the library and **Edit a copy** (and **Update to N** when a newer release of the library exists, which opens its update review in Assets), which gives the project its own editable copy (it no longer receives the library's updates); you can undo it. If the file has a mistake, the pages are read-only until it is fixed: they keep showing the last version that worked, with a warning line naming the line to fix and a **View as file** button. **Change…** beside a colour opens the colour picker: the page previews as you drag and the change is saved when you let go. Click a ramp step to pin it and pick its colour: it is pinned at its colour and the colour picker opens next to the step (the step follows while you drag and saves when you let go; Esc closes the picker and returns to the step); a pinned step shows a reset arrow (**Reset to generated**) that puts the generated colour back. **Where used** (the target button beside **Change…** on colour and role rows) lists the canvases that use the token and selects its layers. The colour card's **…** menu holds **Duplicate**. On **Roles**, **Change…** picks the ramp step a role points at (hovering a step previews it). On **Scales**, type a step's value to pin it; its reset arrow returns the scale's value. Every change keeps the file's comments and layout, can be undone (a toast with **Undo**, or Cmd+Z), and is written to `Styles/default.ds`. Effects are still changed in the `.ds` text (View as file) or by asking the AI. A project with no `.ds` yet shows **No design system yet** with **Open**, which creates the default one.

**From app Settings to a project's settings.** The last group of the Settings rail, **Project settings**, lists every project you have open in a tab, in the order of your tabs (a folder icon, the project's name and an arrow). Click one to switch to that project's tab with its Project tab open on General. The group is hidden while no project is open. In the browser it lists the project open in the editor. (The Project tab's **App settings** row at its foot goes the other way.)

**Publishing a desktop folder.** When a design folder isn't published yet, the Project tab shows the same publish row, reading "Publish" while nothing is live yet, with its checkmark off, and the rest of the flow underneath: (1) name the project (prefilled from the folder name; the `brilliant.design/{handle}/{project}` link previews underneath, and the handle can be changed any time in Settings → Account). A name the server won't take shows a red line under the preview naming what's wrong and what to type instead, such as "Project names are lowercase. Try "my-project"."; (2) choose the visibility it goes live with in the Visibility row: public, or private on paid plans, where the row starts on private (free plans publish as public and the row says so); (3) review "What gets published", a scrollable list naming exactly what will ship, every design (`.bl`) and design-system (`.ds`) file plus the image assets your canvases actually reference (private-looking files like `.env` are flagged, never blocked). Ticking the Publish row is the publish, and it's the only one: there is no separate Publish button. It raises a confirmation toast spelling out what going live means (public: anyone on the internet can see your latest work, live; private: it goes live as a private project and only people you invite can open it), and the publish only starts from that toast's Publish button; the row waits dimmed while the toast is up. A quiet **"Publishing from &lt;folder&gt;"** line at the top of the flow always names the exact folder being published (the scratch sandbox reads "Scratch workspace"), so you can never publish the wrong one by accident. While it runs, a file-by-file progress bar shows under the row **and** a pinned progress notification stays on the rail, so closing and reopening Settings still shows the upload in progress; the same repository can't be published twice at once. Then the first checkpoint is created, the link is copied, and the folder becomes Published. If the name is already taken by a different project of yours, publishing stops with **"You already have a project named "X". Choose a different name."** and writes nothing: it never overwrites an existing project. Only design files and referenced assets ship, never your `.brilliant` folder, git history, or unreferenced files. Visitors of a public project see your latest work live; a Pro option can limit them to checkpoints (the Audience row once published).

**Copying the link when published.** Once published, the copy-link button copies a shareable deep link to the current canvas (selection-aware). If you've edited locally since the last publish, the toast says the link doesn't include your latest changes yet and offers **Publish latest** to push them. On desktop the **Checkpoint** button also pushes the latest work to the public copy (rather than making a local git commit) once the folder is a published project.

**Publishing a drop.** With elements selected, **Publish drop** (palette; right-click the selection on the canvas; or right-click a layer row in the Layers panel; needs a signed-in account) shares the selection as a standalone drop at `brilliant.design/{handle}/drops/{slug}` (older `/drops/{handle}/{slug}` links redirect there). The confirm toast carries three optional fields you can fill inline before pressing Publish (or Enter): **Title** (prefilled from the selection's name; it also seeds the URL slug), **Subtitle**, and **Tags** (chips: type and press comma or Enter to add, up to 5 lowercase-kebab tags such as `dark-mode`; an invalid tag shows the reason under the field). It also carries a **Private** toggle: drops publish **public by default** (they appear on your profile and anyone with the link can view them); flipping Private on publishes the drop **privately**: only people you invite can open it. Private drops are a paid feature, so on a free plan the toggle is locked to public. The text fields are optional: Publish fires immediately with whatever the fields hold, and you can edit them later from the drop's **Settings** tab (there is no separate edit affordance on the drop's page or its tile), including switching a drop between public and private. On success the drop's link lands on the clipboard. **Import drop from link** pastes a copied drop URL's contents onto the current canvas. Right-clicking from the **Layers** panel is often easier than the canvas when you want a whole parent frame, because on the canvas the click lands on whichever child sits under the cursor, while a layer row targets exactly the layer you clicked.

**Publishing part of a project as its own project.** **Publish as new project** (palette; or right-click an element selection; or right-click one or more canvases in the file explorer) makes a brand-new project out of just that subset instead of the whole workspace. The route picks the subset: the selected elements, the canvases you right-clicked, or, run with nothing selected, the current canvas. It raises a "Create a new project from this?" confirm with a name field. The name can be anything you like: type it however reads best, spaces and capitals and all. A small preview under the field shows the dashed web address it becomes (so "My Cool Project 2" previews as `my-cool-project-2`); the only names it won't take are an empty one and one made purely of symbols (which leaves no address), and it says so. On confirm the new project is created and opened in its own tab. On desktop it is created on your device first, and publishing it to the cloud (and picking its visibility) is then the usual explicit act on that new project's Project tab in Settings (if your account connection isn't ready yet, it tells you the project was created locally and points you to finish). This needs a signed-in account, but you don't have to sign in first: running it signed out opens the sign-in door and then continues, so it never dead-ends. It is separate from publishing the whole folder and from a drop, giving a slice of your work a full project of its own.
